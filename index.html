Daniel Dominguez, the independent developer of Ironstride (“we”, “us” or “our”), makes Ironstride, an iPhone app for lifting and running programs (on the App Store: “Ironstride: Lift & Run Plan”; “the app”). This Privacy Policy describes how we process personal information in connection with the app, this web page, and support messages you send us (together, the “Service”).

State privacy rights notice: see State privacy rights notice below for your rights under U.S. state privacy laws. Notice to European users: see Notice to European users for additional information for individuals in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom (“Europe”).

Summary

  • The app collects nothing. It has no accounts, no ads, no analytics or tracking code, and no network code: it never connects to the internet, and nothing you enter or log in the app is sent to us or to anyone else by the app.
  • Your answers, programs, workouts and runs, including runs read from Apple Health if you allow it, stay on your iPhone. We never receive them.
  • The only personal information we ever receive is what you choose to send us by email (for example, a support question).
  • We don’t sell or share personal information, and we don’t use it for advertising.

Personal information we collect

Through the app: none

The app works entirely on your iPhone. What you enter is kept in the app’s own storage on your iPhone, to build your plan and show your training back to you:

  • Your setup answers: what you train for, your sex, birth year, height, weight and goal weight, your lifting experience, how active you are, where you train and how many days a week, and, if you run, how much you run now, your longest recent run, a race day and a recent race time if you give them. You can change them later.
  • Your training: the programs and running plans you start and the days you choose, the workouts you log (each set’s exercise, weight, reps or time held) and how hard each felt, the runs you log or that are read from Apple Health (distance, time and how they felt), and changes you make to your plan, such as swapping an exercise or skipping a missed day.
  • A few settings: your units (lb or kg, mi or km), whether you’ve seen the intro, when you agreed to the health notice, and whether Ironstride reads Apple Health (with where its last read reached and the runs you removed), kept in the app’s preferences on your iPhone.

None of this is sent to us. Your iPhone’s own backups (to iCloud or to a computer) include the app’s data like any other app’s, under Apple’s terms. Deleting the app deletes its data from your iPhone.

Besides Apple Health (above), the app asks iOS for one permission, which you can change at any time in Settings › Notifications › Ironstride:

  • Notifications (optional): asked when you start your first workout, and used only to tell you your rest between sets is over while the app isn’t in front. The alert is made on your iPhone.

Apple Health

If you run, Ironstride can read the runs you record (with Apple Fitness, a watch or another app) from Apple Health, so it can mark your planned runs as done and add up your week. It asks first, and iOS asks you which data it may read; you can change that at any time in the Health app, among the apps it shares data with, and turn reading off in Ironstride’s Settings.

  • What it reads: your running workouts: when each started and ended, how long you ran, and its distance. Nothing else from Apple Health, and it writes nothing there.
  • Where it goes: each run read is kept in the app’s storage on your iPhone, like a run you log by hand. It is never sent to us or anyone else, never used for advertising, and never stored in iCloud by the app.
  • Turning reading off keeps the runs already read; you can remove any of them in the app. Deleting the app deletes them.

Health and safety

Before the app gives you any training, it shows this notice, which you agree to and can read again in the app (Settings › Health and safety):

  • Ironstride gives general fitness information. It is not medical advice, and it does not replace a doctor, a physical therapist or a trainer who knows you.
  • Talk to a doctor before you start this or any exercise program, above all if you are pregnant, are coming back from an injury or surgery, or have a heart condition, high blood pressure or any other health condition.
  • Stop and get medical help if you feel chest pain, feel faint or dizzy, cannot catch your breath, or feel sharp or sudden pain. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
  • Exercise can cause injury. The app suggests weights and targets by rules. You decide what you lift and how hard you push, and you train at your own risk.
